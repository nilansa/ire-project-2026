## Original problem and present alternative

The [Microsoft Research fellowship research challenges](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/microsoft-research-fellowship/research-challenges/) proposal on efficient and accurate post-training asks how feedback selection, loss design and compute can improve retrieval. The repository's [research plan](../../RESEARCH_PLAN.md) frames this as candidate search, expensive feedback and retriever post-training. The current frozen-weight, inference-only study is an alternative research direction; it does not by itself answer the post-training question.

The attached [arXiv:2409.17424v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17424v1) is *Results of the Big ANN: NeurIPS'23 competition*, not DISCo.

## Inference-time feedback: prior work and claim boundary

- [TOUR, ACL Findings 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.354/) and its [released code](https://github.com/dmis-lab/TouR) optimize test-time query representations with relevance feedback. The paper and released phrase code do not use one universally identical soft-label stopping path; declare which path is used. The completed single-step MiniLM/MedCPT study with 20 feedback documents is TOUR-inspired transfer, not a reproduction of the DPR/depth-100 paper setup.
- [ReFIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11744) studies reranker feedback during inference; the [large-scale IBM study, SIGIR 2025](https://research.ibm.com/publications/a-large-scale-study-of-reranker-relevance-feedback-at-inference) examines reranker-guided query distillation, including feedback depth, update count and latency.
- [TARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12487), with [IBM's released code](https://github.com/IBM/task-aware-embedding-refinement), uses language-model feedback to refine query embeddings and reports Adam optimization. [TTT-Embed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12569), also listed on its [author's publications page](https://tianyucodings.github.io/publications/), learns embedding-space vectors from ranking rewards, including query-specific and shared scopes; it reports Adam steps. These are close prior approaches, with different feedback, objectives and deployment scopes.

Frozen-model query feedback and adaptive optimization already exist. H2's local-covariance direction is a narrow, testable hypothesis, not a global novelty claim. A mechanism claim would require strong matched controls, including Adam, multiple steps and a diagonal-Fisher comparator, alongside same-gradient, same-applied-displacement and full cost accounting. Uniform initial-50 covariance is not generally the exact Fisher; see the framework's fixed-support derivation. The bounded study was independently reviewed and completed; the tested H2 direction failed its prespecified practical development screen. Broad mechanism or novelty claims remain unsupported, and Adam, multiple steps and diagonal Fisher remain future controls.

## Collective retrieval is a different question

[DISCo, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/6ded182245f8e4db3963d2027174133d-Abstract-Conference.html) seeks a small set of corpus items whose contextual word vectors collectively cover contextual query vectors. It formulates coverage as a submodular objective and uses dense index probes, including its LiftedRP construction. This is contextual token/atom coverage, not n-gram coverage and not an identical objective to H2's single-query covariance direction. The existing [DISCo diagnostic](../2026-11-15-disco-rank-vs-coverage-diagnostic.md) and tracker should be read before suggesting another experiment.
